Multi-currency accounts, wealth and transfers

One workspace, many currencies. Every account keeps its own money; every total says what it is worth, at which rate, and what it had to leave out.

1. What a user gets

A person with money in Italy and India adds a euro account and a rupee account to the same workspace. Nothing else about the app changes: the euro account is in euros, the rupee account is in rupees, and the totals are in whichever currency they chose to read in.

The one rule everything follows

A native balance is a fact. ₹75,000 stays ₹75,000 tomorrow. A converted value is an approximation at a stated rate on a stated date, and the screen says so in words. If a currency has no rate, it is listed natively and excluded from the total, with the exclusion named. Nothing is ever silently counted as 1:1.

2. Adding money in a foreign account

The transaction form follows the account you pick. Choose the rupee wallet and the amount field asks for rupees; every account in the list shows its own balance in its own currency.

A split stays in one currency

A split is one purchase paid from several accounts, so it is one amount — and amounts in different currencies cannot be added. Picking a second account in another currency is refused with “Same currency as USD only” rather than producing a meaningless total.

3. Moving money between currencies

The transfer form asks what leaves and what arrives. It fills the arrival in for you at today's market rate, and then gets out of the way: banks rarely give the market rate, so the moment you edit the figure it is yours, and what the ledger stores is what you actually received.

The principal is never spending. Only the fee is. Both native amounts and the rate actually obtained are stored on the transfer and never recalculated from a later rate.

4. Reports

Every report converts each row at its own date, not at today's rate, so a chart of last year does not move when the rate moves this morning. Each response names the currency it is in and how many rows it could not convert; the screen says so in one calm line rather than under-reporting in silence.

5. How it is built

Account currency
wealth_accounts.currency_code — set at creation, locked once the account has history
Row currency
transactions.currency_code — snapshotted from the account on every write
Reporting currency
organizations.reporting_currency; currency stays as the compatibility alias
Rates
fx_rate_snapshots — one row per pair per day, with provider and source; weekends carried forward and flagged
Providers
Frankfurter (European Central Bank, has history) then open.er-api for pairs the ECB does not publish, such as AED and SAR
Conversion
SQL functions fx_rate_on() and reporting_amount() (migration 0074), so every aggregate converts per row without a join
Transfers
transfers — one logical row above the two legs, holding both native amounts, the effective rate and the fee
Arithmetic
decimal.js throughout the money paths; no binary floating point in the ledger

New API

Privacy

A rate provider receives a currency pair and a date. It never receives an amount, an account name or a description. Conversion happens inside ProfitSync or in the database.

Two behaviours worth a decision

A reversed transfer cannot be trashed. By design: trashing one half of a reversal pair would leave balances wrong. The consequence is that an account which took part in one can only be archived, never deleted. Reasonable for an audit trail, but it is a product choice, not an accident.

Extra cash wallets used to be undeletable. The old guard refused to delete any cash account, which was correct when a workspace had exactly one. Now that a workspace can hold one wallet per currency, only the auto-provisioned “Cash in Hand” is permanent. Fixed here.

What the money tests pin

  • An account's native balance does not move when a rate moves.
  • A cross-currency transfer keeps both native amounts and its own effective rate.
  • The fee is the only expense; the principal is never spending; a transfer is never income.
  • Reversal restores both sides exactly once and refuses a second time.
  • A planned or cancelled transfer moves no money; completing one moves it exactly once.
  • Net worth is the sum of converted balances, never of raw numbers across currencies.

8. Not built yet

  • Destination-side fees. Only a source-side fee is modelled.
  • Historical net-worth chart. The per-day rate snapshots exist; no chart values balances at each point's own date yet.
  • Currency-movement attribution — separating “I saved” from “the rate moved”. Designed for, not built.
  • Cross-currency recurring transfers are refused with a clear message until they carry an explicit rate policy.
  • A foreign key on clients.organization_id, which has none; the dev database holds ten orphaned client rows. Unrelated to this feature.
